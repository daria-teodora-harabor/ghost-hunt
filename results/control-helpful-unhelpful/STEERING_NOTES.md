# Steering-coefficient sweep

Generated by `scripts/steering_sweep.py`. Different kind of experiment from the rest
of this folder: not reading the direction off activations, but adding it INTO the
residual stream during generation (the standard "activation addition" / steering-
vector technique) and asking how far you can push it before output stops being
language.

## Method, and what we didn't use

- **Steering vector:** the raw, unstandardized version of the same contrastive
  direction (`mean(activation | forced dangerous) - mean(activation | forced safe)`
  at layer 21, no per-dimension rescaling), unit-normalized. This is deliberately
  NOT the saved `probe_layer21.npz` weights, which live in *standardized* (z-scored)
  space — adding a z-scored direction straight into the raw residual stream would
  weight dimensions by 1/std, which is the wrong thing to inject.
- **Coefficient units:** multiples of the typical layer-21 activation norm (~793,
  measured from the 16 benign reference prompts) rather than a raw scalar, so
  "2x typical size" means the same thing at any layer.
- **Coherence metrics — two, because either alone is misleading:**
  1. *Self-perplexity*: teacher-force the generated text back through the SAME
     model with steering turned OFF, and score it there. Asks "does the unsteered
     model find this text plausible."
  2. *Distinct-2 ratio*: unique bigrams / total bigrams. Catches degenerate
     repetition loops that perplexity alone can miss (see result below — this
     wasn't a hypothetical concern, it happened).
  - **Considered and skipped for this pass:** KL divergence between steered and
    unsteered next-token distributions. Cheaper (one forward pass, no generation
    loop) and would give a smoother curve, but doesn't hand back readable samples
    or directly answer "is this nonsense." Worth adding if a finer-grained
    transition point is needed later.
- 4 fixed benign prompts, greedy decoding, 40 new tokens, coefficients swept at
  {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8} x the typical norm.

## Result

| coefficient | mean self-perplexity | mean distinct-2 | what the text looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (baseline) | 1.1 | 1.00 | normal, on-topic answers |
| 0.25x | 1.2 | 0.99 | normal |
| 0.5x | 2.8 | 0.97 | normal, slightly more hedged phrasing |
| **1.0x** | **53.4** | **0.61** | **breaks down: hedging, self-interruption, confusion** |
| 2.0x | 5.3 | 0.06 | full collapse: repeats "Maybe.1.1.1.1.1.1..." |
| 4.0x | 5.7 | 0.05 | full collapse: "1.1.1.1.1.1..." |
| 8.0x | 4.0 | 0.03 | full collapse: "sometimes sometimes sometimes..." |

**Perplexity is not monotonic — read distinct-2 alongside it, not perplexity alone.**
It peaks around 1.0x and then falls back down at 2x-8x, even though the text at 2x-8x
is obviously worse nonsense than at 1x. The reason: once generation collapses into
repeating one token or phrase, that repetition becomes highly predictable (even to
the unsteered model — "the next token is very likely to be the same token again"),
so perplexity drops even as content quality is at its lowest. This is exactly the
failure mode the method note above flagged before running anything, and it's why
distinct-2 is here: it falls monotonically and unambiguously across the whole range
(1.00 -> 0.03), correctly identifying total collapse where perplexity's U-shape would
be misread as "getting better again."

**Recommended reading:**
- **≤0.5x typical norm: safe, stays coherent and on-topic.**
- **~1.0x: the transition zone** — text becomes confused and self-interrupting
  ("Wait, the text is not complete... Maybe you could try this? Wait, maybe...").
  Worth noting honestly: this reads as *disoriented*, not as a clean shift toward
  dishonest or unhelpful CONTENT. Don't over-read this as "the model became
  deceptive" — it's closer to "the intervention is large enough to disrupt normal
  generation," which is a coherence effect, not (yet) evidence of a meaningful
  behavioral shift along the intended axis.
- **≥2.0x: full collapse** into degenerate repetition. Not usable for anything.

## Caveats

- 4 prompts, all benign/factual — no attempt yet to see whether steering toward
  "unhelpful" surfaces anything resembling the actual honesty-flavored content the
  probe direction is built from (e.g. hedged refusals, admissions) before hitting
  outright incoherence. The transition zone (~1.0x) is the place to look closely at
  more samples and more prompts if that's the next question.
- Single layer (21), single model (the clean control organism). Whether the same
  coefficient scale holds at other layers, or on the eventual backdoored organism,
  is untested.
- Greedy decoding only — sampling might reveal a different (or wider) transition
  zone.
